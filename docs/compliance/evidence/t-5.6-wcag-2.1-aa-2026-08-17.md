low — they render from the same layouts and palette as the sixteen that were

the gate should not be signed as though they had been looked at.

## What the first run found, and what was done

The opening scan reported **254 colour-contrast failures plus four structural
issues**. All are closed.

### Structural

| Issue | Impact | Where | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| `select-name` | **critical** | Admin → Grievances filter bar | The category and escalation selects had no accessible name. A screen reader announced "combo box" and nothing about what it filtered. Added `aria-label` to both, and to the search input — a placeholder is not a label. |
| `aria-prohibited-attr` | serious | Paginator, 3 admin pages | Laravel's default paginator puts `aria-disabled` and `aria-label` on a bare `<span>`, which has no implicit role for either to attach to, so both are dropped. Published the view and added `role="link"`, which is the pairing ARIA actually permits. |
| `aria-prohibited-attr` | serious | Income tab strip | Same defect on the "Awards & Rewards (Coming soon)" span. The attribute is gone; the visible "(Coming soon)" text is what carries the meaning, for everybody. |
| `meta-viewport`, `button-name` | moderate / critical | — | Artefacts of a 500 page. See below. |

### The 500 that was not a real finding

The first run scanned Laravel's error page for `/admin/grievances` and dutifully
reported its clipboard button and `user-scalable=no` viewport. The dev database
was several migrations behind, so the page was throwing. Worth recording only
as a caution: **an accessibility scanner will happily audit your error page and
report the framework's markup as your own.** Check the pass/fail reason before
acting on a violation.

### Colour contrast — 254 → 0

All of it was four palette choices. The fix uses darker tokens from the same
brand families already defined in `resources/css/app.css`; no new colours were
introduced.

| Foreground | Background | Was | Now | Nodes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `#008cc7` brand-600 text | white | 3.76:1 | brand-700 `#0a719f` — 5.3:1 | 151 |
| white | `#00b6ef` brand-500 fill | 2.34:1 | brand-700 fill — 5.3:1 | 54 |
| `#99a1af` gray-400 text | white | 2.60:1 | gray-600 `#4a5565` — 7.0:1 | 30 |
| white | `#f5922a` sunrise-500 badge | 2.32:1 | sunrise-800 `#8f4b11` — 7.4:1 | 9 |

A second pass cleared the tail: sunrise-700 turned out to be 4.46:1 with white
text — four hundredths under the threshold, which is exactly the kind of near
miss that gets waved through by eye — so filled sunrise chips went to
sunrise-800. `slate-400`, `slate-200` and `gray-500` were failing because they
sit on the admin page background `#f4f7f6` rather than on a dark panel;
`amber-600` on white was 3.19:1; and a `bg-white/25` count badge left white
text at 3.39:1 over the brand fill, replaced with a solid `brand-900` chip.

Totals changed: 249 link/text classes, 143 filled buttons, 281 + 1,029 muted
text classes, 18 badges, 10 amber, 74 slate. Assets rebuilt.

## What this scan does not cover

- **Automated scanning finds roughly a third of WCAG issues.** Zero axe
  violations is not the same as accessible. Keyboard-only navigation, focus
  order, screen-reader flow through the registration wizard, and whether error
  messages are announced were not tested here and want a manual pass.
- **One browser, one viewport** — Chromium at desktop size. Mobile layout and
  Safari/VoiceOver were not exercised.
- **The eight distributor pages**, as above.

## Re-running it

```bash
npm i -D @axe-core/playwright          # already in package.json
npx playwright test tests/Browser/accessibility.spec.js
```

The spec fails the build on any violation, so it can go into CI as a
regression gate. It is the palette that will drift first: a new
`text-brand-600` or `bg-brand-500 text-white` anywhere reintroduces the
problem, and only this scan will notice.
